# 1. Source Basis and Contribution Boundary

This section is based on the SEN381 CivicConnect Master Project Brief and the Milestone 1 brief. The Master Brief defines CivicConnect as a community service request management platform and describes the current fragmented request process, the minimum requester/staff/management capabilities, and the mandatory project constraints (Belgium Campus ITversity, 2026a).

No stakeholder interview evidence has been supplied at this stage. Therefore, stakeholder needs that come directly from the brief are treated as source-backed, while additional lifecycle stakeholders and influence/interest ratings are clearly identified as team analysis rather than interviewed fact.

# 2. Problem and Business Need

## 2.1 Problem Analysis

The organisation currently receives service requests through a fragmented combination of email, telephone calls, WhatsApp messages, spreadsheets and paper records. This creates recurring problems such as duplicate or lost requests, weak assignment and ownership, limited status visibility for requesters, inconsistent reporting, weak accountability for status/action changes, and inconsistent handling of sensitive information. The core engineering problem is therefore the absence of one controlled, traceable record of the service-request lifecycle rather than merely the absence of a new user interface (Belgium Campus ITversity, 2026a).

| Observed problem | Engineering implication |
|---|---|
| Requests may be duplicated, overlooked, incorrectly assigned or lost. | CivicConnect must support controlled request capture and clear ownership. |
| Requesters have limited visibility of request progress. | Requester-facing status, history and meaningful feedback are required business capabilities. |
| Staff struggle to prioritise, coordinate and identify ownership. | Authorised staff need useful request views, search/filter/sort and responsibility control. |
| Status/action accountability is weak. | The solution must preserve a controlled lifecycle and traceable actions. |
| Management information is unreliable or manual. | Structured request information must support oversight of open, overdue, resolved and closed work. |
| Sensitive information may be handled inconsistently. | Security and authorised access must be considered from the beginning of the lifecycle. |

## 2.2 Business Need and Intended Value

The business need is for a controlled digital platform that provides a reliable, traceable and usable way to submit, manage, monitor and report service requests, while avoiding an unsustainable technical, operational or financial burden (Belgium Campus ITversity, 2026a).

| Value area | Expected value |
|---|---|
| Visibility | Requesters can determine whether their requests were received and what the current state is. |
| Accountability | Responsibility, actions and lifecycle changes are easier to trace. |
| Operational control | Staff can identify relevant work and ownership using consistent request information. |
| Management oversight | Open, overdue, resolved and closed requests can be identified from controlled records. |
| Information quality | A single controlled lifecycle record reduces fragmented and inconsistent reporting. |
| Sustainability | Scope and later technology decisions remain constrained by team capacity, cost, quality, security and operational needs. |
