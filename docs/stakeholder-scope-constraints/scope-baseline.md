# 4. Scope Baseline

The Master Brief provides minimum business capabilities but states that these are not complete detailed requirements. The scope below converts those capabilities into a controlled M1 boundary and deliberately avoids committing the team to additional integrations or technology choices before evidence exists (Belgium Campus ITversity, 2026a; Belgium Campus ITversity, 2026b).

## 4.1 In Scope

- Direct submission of service requests into CivicConnect with appropriate request information.
- Selection of a request category through a controlled category mechanism.
- Requester access to the current status and history/list of previously submitted requests.
- Meaningful requester feedback when a request is accepted, rejected, updated or completed.
- Authorised staff access to relevant service requests, including search, filtering, sorting and full request details.
- Assignment or acceptance of responsibility for a request.
- Controlled request status updates and recording of relevant actions, comments or resolution information.
- Authorised resolution and closure of service requests.
- Management/oversight views that identify open, overdue, resolved and closed work and support category/status analysis.

## 4.2 Out of Scope for the Initial Baseline

- Automatic ingestion or synchronisation of requests from email, telephone calls, WhatsApp, spreadsheets or paper records. These channels describe the current problem, but direct integration is not a minimum capability.
- Payments, inventory, asset-management or unrelated business modules outside the service-request problem.
- AI chatbot or autonomous AI classification/decision-making. These are not minimum capabilities and would create additional accuracy, privacy, security and verification obligations.

## 4.3 Deferred / Future Scope

- External email/SMS/WhatsApp notifications, subject to later cost, privacy, integration and operational evidence.
- File/photo attachments, subject to storage, privacy, malware-scanning and cost analysis.
- Advanced analytics, data exports, automated escalation and advanced service-level management are deferred until detailed management rules and stakeholder value are validated. A basic rule for determining whether a request is overdue remains within the baseline because overdue request identification is a minimum management capability.
- Third-party integrations and single sign-on after organisational systems and technology/environment constraints are known.
- Delivery platform implementation, including whether CivicConnect will use a web-based, native mobile or other client interface, is deferred until evidence-based technology and interface decisions are made in a later milestone.

## 4.4 Deliberate Exclusion to Defend

Automatic legacy-channel integration is deliberately excluded from the initial baseline.

The current fragmented process uses email, telephone, WhatsApp, spreadsheets and paper, but the minimum CivicConnect capabilities require the platform to support request submission and lifecycle management; they do not require automatic ingestion from those channels. Including such integrations now would introduce third-party dependencies, duplicate-handling rules, privacy/security concerns, additional testing and schedule risk. A direct CivicConnect submission path therefore provides the required business capability while keeping the initial scope controllable. The team may reconsider integrations later through formal impact analysis if stakeholder value justifies them.
